# Codex Sepuricus Meditations

by

May we all meet up again, in another life.

Rainy day. Thunderclouds miles off
boom and thunder. Tear drops of rain
explode on the grey sands.
splashes of blood mixed with water.
Thuds of large sailors collapsing under
weapons. 
Bronze and iron clatter like thunderclaps
as another young sailor gets his head
smashed in by
my double mace.
Grinning, soaked, covered in gore
the brothers Temprus laugh and move on.
Broken bones, snapped necks and twisted fingers.
The rain carries away the filth, washing twisted 
and rotten 
docks 
clean of filth.
Silent pie eating in a lean-to.
No words spoken while counting loot.
Sleep now, there will be more violence tomorrow.
Remarks on a silly person
You sir, thinking my name outloud. 
Have We met before?
In a past life, perhaps.
Hmm, you pretend like we're old friends, 
yet we have only just met.
So many friends leave Iridine
I become wary of trusting new friends.
one talent loaned,
zero sens returned.
Lost profits and wasted time.
I stopped hunting rats years ago.
Never again.

A poem for mother

If I had the time,
I would have been there to stop dad from leaving.
If I was stronger, I would have held the family together
with my arms.
If I was braver, I wouldn't have ran.
If I could have turned back time,
tipped the hourglass on its end.
IF only I could convince you to run 
to follow
to leave.
Your strength is the hardest lesson to learn.
I stared into the face of death and ran.
Ran past little brother crying in his crib,
past aunts and uncles laying in pools of blood.
I ran past you mother.
I ran and I never looked back.
I can never go back, but look forward.
Become dangerous and grab vengeance from
the heavens
for myself and our clan.
To you I dedicate this poem.
To our clan, I dedicate my life.
One day things will be right.

Lately, while I was lounging, a funny thought did I think.
"Hmm," I had said, and "hmm." I replied.
A quandary like a small spark of inspiration.
An idea, an image, an instant.
Gone as quickly as it came, as if a thief stole it from my tongue.
Dumbfounded, I soak myself in cold water and meditate.
Through low hums and prayers, I coax another timid thought from hiding.
Some old idea, some old story stowed away in a dusty room in a broken down crate.
Yes, and what if?
Can it be done? Should it?
Who knows. Not me, I've just thought of it.
Eyelids grow heavy, and the stomach rumbles.
Reluctantly, I stand. Arms waving notions to the wind.
One thought remains. Food. 
And from one thought, millions are created.
Which type of pie?
Shall I eat it fast?
Does brother deserve one?
In that case, what have I done to deserve one?
Who's payin?
Who can we con into payin?
Too much planning, not enough pie.
