# Jewelry Guide

Jewelry Crafting allows characters to create jewelry from a variety of metals, cut and polish gemstones, engrave finished pieces, and set gemstones into compatible jewelry.

The system is made up of several related skills. Different recipes use different combinations of those skills, and **only the skills actually used during the creation of an item contribute to its finished quality**. As a result, mastering one type of jewelry does not necessarily require mastering every skill in the skillset.


![](/assets/wikidot/jewelry-guide/gemcuts2.png)


### How Jewelry Is Made

Jewelry recipes do not all follow the same production process.

The skills that affect the finished quality of a piece are **only those actually used by that recipe**.

For example, Assembly does not affect a forged ring unless that specific recipe contains an assembly step.

Broadly, jewelry production falls into several different processes.


#### Cast Jewelry

Cast jewelry begins with a wax form.

The general process is:

# Form the wax piece.
# Create a clay mold from the wax form.
# Bake the mold.
# Smelt the required metal.
# Cast the metal into the prepared mold.
# Perform any additional recipe steps.
# Optionally engrave the finished jewelry.
# Optionally set compatible gemstones.


#### Forged Jewelry

Other jewelry is produced by working metal stock rather than pouring molten metal into a mold.

The exact steps depend upon the recipe and may involve:

# Hot-working metal into the required stock.
# Cold-working the stock into components.
# Assembling components when required.
# Engraving the finished piece, if supported.
# Setting gemstones, if supported.


#### Gem Cutting

Gem cutting is its own multi-step process.

The general process is:

# Layout the intended cut.
# Rough cut the stone.
# Shape the required facets.
# Polish the finished gemstone.

Different cuts require different numbers of Rough Cut and Shape actions.

### Gem Quality

Gem value and finished quality are influenced by several different factors.

* Gem type
* Gem size
* Underlying quality and clarity
* Cut
* Skill of the gem cutter

Known sizes include:

* Tiny
* Small
* Large
* Enormous

Size alone does not determine value. Two gems of the same type and size can have very different appraised values because of their underlying quality. Merchant's Eye is highly recommended for a dedicated jeweler.

Cutting a gemstone increases its value.

### Finished Jewelry Quality

Finished jewelry quality is dependent upon **every skill that actually touches the creation process**.

Skills which are not used in a recipe do not contribute to the quality of that item.

For example:

* Casting matters when an item is cast.
* Cold Work matters when the recipe uses cold working.
* Assembly matters only when the recipe contains an assembly step.
* Gem-cutting skills affect the gemstone rather than an unrelated metalworking step.
* Engraving affects the finished item only when it is engraved.
* Setting affects the finished item when a gemstone is set.

This means there is no single universal Jewelry Crafting quality skill. The important skills depend upon the exact recipe and finishing steps used.

Nikolah (GM) described Jewelry Crafting as intentionally requiring **true mastery and combat-like ranks** to create top-end items.

#### Engraving

Engraving directly increases an item's value.

Even relatively inexperienced engravers can add value.

Engraving also provides a rank-based quality bonus. According to GM clarification, the quality bonus from engraving is approximately half of the benefit available from setting gemstones.

#### Setting Gemstones

Setting a gemstone generally adds approximately the gemstone's existing value to the jewelry.

Setting can also improve the finished jewelry's quality based upon:

* the quality of the stone
* the skill of the setter

Compatible jewelry may support anywhere from one to ten gemstones, depending upon the recipe.

### Jewelry Value

Finished jewelry value is cumulative.

The metal item establishes the base value, engraving can increase it, and successfully set gemstones add further value.

In general:

**Metal Item + Engraving + Gemstones = Finished Value**

This makes expensive metals, good engraving, and high-value gems especially important when producing jewelry for sale rather than simply training the skills.

Higher-value metals can be substantially more profitable, but also require greater skill to work reliably. Material loss should therefore be considered when choosing what metal to use for training versus production.
